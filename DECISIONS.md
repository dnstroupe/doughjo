# Design decisions

The main question: can an assortment feel playful while the customer understands its contents, total, and available changes?

- Six or twelve gives the box a clear completion point. Every donut retains its individual price.
- Duplicates are allowed. A collection should not force variety.
- A replacement keeps the original until the customer selects something else.
- Explicit movement controls avoid requiring precise drag gestures.
- Removing a donut and shrinking a box offer Undo.
- A completed box unlocks reveal and checkout. A sold out flavor blocks progress until addressed.
- The total uses integer cents and explicitly labeled fictional six percent tax. No pickup fee.
- The flavor finder is a direct taste filter, not an invented personality assessment.
- The mascot introduces the brand, helps with flavor discovery, responds to box progress, and celebrates completion. Checkout reduces visual distraction.
- Animation is brief and optional. There is no autoplay carousel or flashing content.
- There is no real payment, inventory, restaurant address, or email integration.

Next evaluation: real users should be able to explain the final cost, change a full box, and understand whether an order exists after a failed attempt. These are planned research questions, not findings.

- Detailed product art stays consistent from discovery to review.
- Drinks are optional, separately counted, and priced before checkout.
- Suggested squads are transparent starting points, not personalized recommendations. Customers review before replacing their box and can Undo.
- Pairings explain the taste contrast and leave the choice with the customer.
